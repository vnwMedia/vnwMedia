Practice Marketing FAQ

Questions worth answering before the plan begins.

Marketing decisions should support the patient experience and align with the practice’s own legal and compliance guidance.

What marketing channels work best for healthcare practices?+

A clear website, local SEO, accurate listings, helpful service content, reputation management, referral support, and carefully scoped paid search are common foundations. The right mix depends on specialty, patient demand, location, capacity, and restrictions.

Can one website support multiple specialties or locations?+

Yes. A clear architecture can organize services, providers, locations, patient resources, and appointment paths while preserving useful internal connections between them.

How should privacy and HIPAA affect marketing?+

Forms, analytics, advertising tools, vendors, and data flows should be reviewed with the practice’s privacy, security, legal, and compliance teams. We can design a lower-friction experience and help document the marketing technology in scope, but the practice’s advisors determine its requirements.

Should every treatment have its own page?+

Priority services often benefit from complete pages when patients have distinct questions, eligibility considerations, expectations, or next steps. Pages should be useful and specific, not created only to target a keyword.

How can a practice build reputation responsibly?+

Use a consistent, non-coercive process for requesting authentic feedback, respond according to practice policy, and avoid revealing protected or sensitive information. The practice should approve the response protocol.

What should we measure?+

Useful measures can include qualified appointment requests, calls, directions, service interest, new-patient demand, referral actions, and campaign source—using measurement methods approved for the practice.
